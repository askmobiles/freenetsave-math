Multiplying by 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000

Multiplying by 10 makes every digit worth ten times more. The digits shift left, so the decimal point seems to jump right.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Count the zeros. That is how many places the decimal point moves to the right.

  • × 10: one place. 4.5 × 10 = 45.
  • × 100: two places. 0.45 × 100 = 45.
  • × 1,000: three places. 3.2 × 1,000 = 3,200.
  • × 10,000: four places. 5.1 × 10,000 = 51,000.

When you run out of digits, fill the empty places with zeros.

Dividing works the other way: the point moves to the left. 45 ÷ 100 = 0.45.

Check the size. Multiplying by 1,000 should make the number a lot bigger. If it got smaller, the point went the wrong way.

See three worked examples

3.2 × 1,000
  1. 1,000 has three zeros, so move the point 3 places right.
  2. 3.2 → 32. → 320. → 3,200.

Answer: 3,200

0.45 × 100
  1. Two zeros, so 2 places right.
  2. 0.45 → 4.5 → 45.

Answer: 45

7 × 10,000
  1. 7 is 7.0. Four zeros, so 4 places right.
  2. Fill the gaps with zeros.

Answer: 70,000
