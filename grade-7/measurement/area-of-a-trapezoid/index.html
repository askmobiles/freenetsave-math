Area of a trapezoid

Add the two parallel sides, halve it, then multiply by the height.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides (they never meet).

Why does this work? Two copies of the trapezoid, one flipped, make a parallelogram with base (10 + 15) and height 16. The trapezoid is half of that.

Area = (a + b) ÷ 2 × h, where a and b are the parallel sides.

Use the height, not the slanted side. The height goes straight across between the parallel sides.

See three worked examples

Parallel sides 10 and 15, height 16.
  1. (10 + 15) ÷ 2 = 12.5.
  2. 12.5 × 16.

Answer: 200 cm²

Parallel sides 12 and 14, height 6.
  1. (12 + 14) ÷ 2 = 13.
  2. 13 × 6.

Answer: 78 cm²

Parallel sides 6 and 12, height 6.
  1. (6 + 12) ÷ 2 = 9.
  2. 9 × 6.

Answer: 54 cm²
