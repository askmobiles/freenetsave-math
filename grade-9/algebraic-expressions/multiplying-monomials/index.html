Multiplying monomials

To multiply monomials, multiply the numbers and add the exponents of the same variable.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A monomial is one term, like 4x³ or −2a²b. To multiply monomials:

  1. Multiply the coefficients (the numbers), watching the signs.
  2. For each variable, add the exponents: x³ × x⁴ = x⁷, because that is x·x·x times x·x·x·x, seven x's in all.

Dividing works the other way: divide the coefficients and subtract exponents: 12x⁵ ÷ 3x² = 4x³.

A variable with no exponent

x means x¹. So 5x × 2x² = 10x³.

Do not multiply the exponents when multiplying terms: x² × x³ is x⁵, not x⁶.

See three worked examples

(3x²)(4x⁵)
  1. Numbers: 3 × 4 = 12.
  2. x: add exponents, 2 + 5 = 7.

Answer: 12x⁷

(−2a³b)(5ab²)
  1. Numbers: −2 × 5 = −10.
  2. a: a³ × a¹ = a⁴. b: b¹ × b² = b³.

Answer: −10a⁴b³

18x⁶ ÷ (−6x²)
  1. Numbers: 18 ÷ (−6) = −3.
  2. x: subtract exponents, 6 − 2 = 4.

Answer: −3x⁴
