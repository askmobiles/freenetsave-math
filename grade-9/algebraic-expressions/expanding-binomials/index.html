Expanding brackets

Expanding means multiplying out brackets so the expression has no brackets left.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac: the term outside multiplies every term inside. For example, 3(2x − 5) = 6x − 15 and −2(x − 4) = −2x + 8.

Two binomials

To multiply (x + 3)(x + 5), every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. An area model keeps track:

Add the four boxes: x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15.

A shortcut

For (x + a)(x + b), the answer is x² + (a + b)x + ab: the middle number is the two numbers added, and the last number is the two multiplied.

Check signs: (x − 2)(x + 6) = x² + 4x − 12, because −2 + 6 = 4 and −2 × 6 = −12.

See three worked examples

Expand 4(3x − 2) − 2(x + 5).
  1. 4(3x − 2) = 12x − 8.
  2. −2(x + 5) = −2x − 10.
  3. Collect: 12x − 2x − 8 − 10.

Answer: 10x − 18

Expand (x + 4)(x − 7).
  1. x × x = x², x × (−7) = −7x, 4 × x = 4x, 4 × (−7) = −28.
  2. Collect the middle terms: −7x + 4x = −3x.

Answer: x² − 3x − 28

Expand (x − 5)².
  1. (x − 5)² means (x − 5)(x − 5).
  2. Middle: −5 + (−5) = −10. Last: (−5)(−5) = 25.

Answer: x² − 10x + 25
