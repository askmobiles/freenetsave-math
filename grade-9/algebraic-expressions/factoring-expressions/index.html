Factoring expressions

Factoring is expanding in reverse: you write an expression as a product.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Step 1, always: take out the greatest common factor (GCF). Find the biggest number and the most variables that divide every term: 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3).

Check by expanding: 3x(2x + 3) = 6x² + 9x. ✓

Factoring x² + bx + c

Look for two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. For x² + 7x + 12: the pairs that multiply to 12 are 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4. Only 3 + 4 = 7. So x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4).

Factoring turns the four boxes back into the side lengths of the rectangle.

Signs: if c is positive, both numbers have the same sign as b. If c is negative, the numbers have different signs.

See three worked examples

Factor 8a³ − 12a².
  1. GCF of 8 and 12 is 4; both terms have at least a².
  2. Take out 4a²: 8a³ ÷ 4a² = 2a, and −12a² ÷ 4a² = −3.

Answer: 4a²(2a − 3)

Factor x² + 9x + 20.
  1. Pairs for 20: 1 × 20, 2 × 10, 4 × 5.
  2. 4 + 5 = 9.

Answer: (x + 4)(x + 5)

Factor x² − 2x − 15.
  1. c = −15 is negative, so the signs differ.
  2. Pairs: 3 and −5 multiply to −15 and add to −2.

Answer: (x + 3)(x − 5)
