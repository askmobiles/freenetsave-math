Appreciation and depreciation

Items like cars lose value over time (depreciation). Items like houses often gain value (appreciation).

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Depreciation: a loss of value. A new car might lose 15–20% of its value each year.

Appreciation: a gain in value. Land, homes and some collectibles may rise in value.

One year

  • Loses 20%: new value = old × 0.80.
  • Gains 5%: new value = old × 1.05.

Several years

The percent applies to the new value each year, so you multiply again: a $30 000 car losing 20% a year is worth 30 000 × 0.8 × 0.8 = $19 200 after 2 years.

Buying used: because new cars lose the most value early, a 2–3 year old car is often a better deal.

See three worked examples

A $28 000 car loses 15% in one year. Value?
  1. 28 000 × 0.85.

Answer: $23 800

A painting worth $5000 gains 8% in a year. Value?
  1. 5000 × 1.08.

Answer: $5400

A $1200 laptop loses 25% a year. Value after 2 years?
  1. 1200 × 0.75 = 900.
  2. 900 × 0.75.

Answer: $675
