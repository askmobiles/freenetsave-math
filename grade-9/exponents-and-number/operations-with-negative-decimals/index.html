Operations with negative decimals

Negative decimals follow the same sign rules as integers. Work out the size, then decide the sign.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Decimals can be negative, like −2.5 (a temperature, or a drop in a stock price). The sign rules you learned for integers still work.

Adding and subtracting

  • Same signs: add the sizes, keep the sign. −1.2 + (−3.5) = −4.7.
  • Different signs: subtract the sizes, keep the sign of the bigger size. −6.4 + 2.1 = −4.3.
  • Subtracting is adding the opposite: 3.2 − (−1.5) = 3.2 + 1.5 = 4.7.

Multiplying and dividing

Multiply as if both were positive, then use the sign rule: same signs give positive, different signs give negative. −2.5 × 4 = −10 and −0.6 × (−0.5) = 0.3.

Estimate first: −2.5 × 4 should be about −10. If your answer is far from your estimate, check the decimal point.

See three worked examples

−3.7 + 5.2
  1. Different signs: subtract the sizes, 5.2 − 3.7 = 1.5.
  2. The bigger size (5.2) is positive.

Answer: 1.5

−1.8 − 2.6
  1. Subtracting 2.6 is adding −2.6: −1.8 + (−2.6).
  2. Same signs: 1.8 + 2.6 = 4.4, keep the minus.

Answer: −4.4

−0.4 × (−2.5)
  1. 0.4 × 2.5 = 1.
  2. Two negatives make a positive.

Answer: 1
