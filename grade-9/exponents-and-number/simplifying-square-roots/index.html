Simplifying square roots

Simplify a square root by finding the biggest perfect square that divides the number.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

The rule √(a × b) = √a × √b lets you split a root into parts. If one part is a perfect square, it comes out as a whole number.

√50 = √(25 × 2) = √25 × √2 = 5√2.

Perfect squares to look for

4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144 …

Steps

  1. Find the largest perfect square that divides the number.
  2. Split the root.
  3. Take the square root of the perfect square.

√72: 36 divides 72, so √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2. (Using 4 gives 2√18, which still is not finished.)

Check: (6√2)² = 36 × 2 = 72. ✓

See three worked examples

Simplify √12.
  1. 12 = 4 × 3.
  2. √4 = 2.

Answer: 2√3

Simplify √98.
  1. 98 = 49 × 2.
  2. √49 = 7.

Answer: 7√2

Simplify 3√45.
  1. 45 = 9 × 5, so √45 = 3√5.
  2. 3 × 3√5 = 9√5.

Answer: 9√5
