Exponent laws: multiplying and dividing powers

When the bases are the same, multiply powers by adding exponents and divide by subtracting them.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A power like 2⁵ has a base (2) and an exponent (5): 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

Product law

x³ × x⁴ = (x·x·x)(x·x·x·x) = x⁷. Rule: xa × xb = xa+b.

Quotient law

x⁶ ÷ x² = (x·x·x·x·x·x)/(x·x) = x⁴. Rule: xa ÷ xb = xa−b.

Zero exponent

x³ ÷ x³ = 1, but the rule also gives x⁰. So x⁰ = 1 for any x ≠ 0.

Same base only: 2³ × 3² cannot be combined with the laws. Work each one out: 8 × 9 = 72.

See three worked examples

5⁴ × 5³
  1. Same base, so add exponents: 4 + 3 = 7.

Answer: 5⁷

a⁹ ÷ a⁴
  1. Same base, so subtract: 9 − 4 = 5.

Answer: a⁵

(3x⁵)(4x²) ÷ (6x⁴)
  1. Multiply: 12x⁷.
  2. Divide: 12 ÷ 6 = 2, and x⁷ ÷ x⁴ = x³.

Answer: 2x³
