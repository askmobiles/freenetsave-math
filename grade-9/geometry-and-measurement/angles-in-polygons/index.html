Angles inside polygons

The angles inside a polygon with n sides add to (n − 2) × 180°.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

From one corner of a polygon you can draw diagonals that split it into triangles. A polygon with n sides splits into n − 2 triangles, and each triangle has 180°.

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°

Regular polygons

All angles are equal, so each one is the sum ÷ n. A regular hexagon: 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.

Working backwards: if the angles add to 1440°, then (n − 2) × 180 = 1440, so n − 2 = 8 and n = 10.

See three worked examples

Sum of the angles in a heptagon (7 sides)?
  1. (7 − 2) × 180 = 5 × 180.

Answer: 900°

Each angle of a regular octagon?
  1. Sum: 6 × 180 = 1080°.
  2. 1080 ÷ 8 = 135.

Answer: 135°

A pentagon has angles 100°, 110°, 120° and 95°. Find the fifth.
  1. Sum is 540°.
  2. 540 − (100 + 110 + 120 + 95) = 540 − 425.

Answer: 115°
