Volume of pyramids

A pyramid holds exactly one third as much as a prism with the same base and height.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Fill a pyramid with water three times and pour it into a box with the same base and height: the box is exactly full.

V = (base area × height) ÷ 3

The height is the straight-up distance from the base to the top point (the apex), not the slanted edge.

Square base 6 m, height 10 m: V = 36 × 10 ÷ 3 = 120 m³.

Same idea for cones: a cone is one third of a cylinder: V = πr²h ÷ 3.

See three worked examples

Square base 3, height 4
  1. Base area 9.
  2. 9 × 4 ÷ 3.

Answer: 12 cubic units

Rectangular base 5 by 6, height 7
  1. Base area 30.
  2. 30 × 7 ÷ 3.

Answer: 70 cubic units

A pyramid has volume 200 cm³ and base area 50 cm². How tall is it?
  1. 200 = 50 × h ÷ 3.
  2. 600 = 50h, so h = 12.

Answer: 12 cm
