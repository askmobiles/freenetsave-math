Slope of a line

Slope measures how steep a line is: how much it rises for each step to the right.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Slope = rise ÷ run. Between two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

Here the slope is 3 ÷ 6 = ½: the line goes up 1 for every 2 to the right.

  • Positive slope: rises to the right. Negative slope: falls to the right.
  • Zero slope: a horizontal line. A vertical line has an undefined slope (you would divide by 0).

In real life slope is a rate of change: a slope of 15 on a graph of dollars against hours means $15 per hour.

See three worked examples

Find the slope between (1, 2) and (4, 11).
  1. Rise: 11 − 2 = 9. Run: 4 − 1 = 3.
  2. m = 9 ÷ 3.

Answer: m = 3

Find the slope between (−2, 5) and (2, −3).
  1. Rise: −3 − 5 = −8. Run: 2 − (−2) = 4.
  2. m = −8 ÷ 4.

Answer: m = −2

A road rises 12 m over a horizontal distance of 200 m. What is its slope as a percent?
  1. m = 12 ÷ 200 = 0.06.
  2. As a percent: 6%.

Answer: 6% grade
