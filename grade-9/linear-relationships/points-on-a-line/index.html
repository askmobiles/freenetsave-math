Points on a line: y = mx + b

Every point on a line makes its equation true. Put in x, and the equation tells you y.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A linear relation makes a straight-line graph. Its equation can be written y = mx + b, where m is the slope (how steep) and b is the y-intercept (where it crosses the y-axis).

Finding points

Choose an x, substitute, and calculate y. For y = 2x − 1: x = 3 gives y = 2(3) − 1 = 5, so (3, 5) is on the line.

Is a point on the line?

Substitute both numbers. (4, 6) is not on y = 2x − 1, because 2(4) − 1 = 7, not 6.

Table of values: pick 3 or more x values, find each y, plot the points and join them with a ruler.

See three worked examples

For y = −3x + 4, find y when x = 2.
  1. Substitute: y = −3(2) + 4.
  2. y = −6 + 4 = −2.

Answer: (2, −2)

Is (−1, 7) on y = −3x + 4?
  1. Substitute x = −1: −3(−1) + 4 = 3 + 4 = 7.
  2. That matches y = 7.

Answer: yes

A plumber charges C = 60h + 80 dollars for h hours. What is the cost for 3 hours?
  1. Substitute h = 3: C = 60(3) + 80.
  2. C = 180 + 80 = 260.

Answer: $260
