Solving two equations together

Two equations with two unknowns can be solved together. The answer is the point where their lines cross.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A system of two equations, like x + y = 10 and x − y = 2, asks for one x and one y that make both true.

Elimination by adding

If one variable has opposite coefficients, add the equations and it disappears:

  • (x + y) + (x − y) = 10 + 2, so 2x = 12 and x = 6.
  • Put x = 6 into x + y = 10: y = 4.

What it looks like

Each equation is a straight line. The solution (6, 4) is the one point on both lines.

Always check in both equations: 6 + 4 = 10 ✓ and 6 − 4 = 2 ✓.

See three worked examples

Solve x + y = 9 and x − y = 3.
  1. Add: 2x = 12, so x = 6.
  2. Substitute: 6 + y = 9, so y = 3.
  3. Check: 6 − 3 = 3. ✓

Answer: x = 6, y = 3

Solve 2x + y = 11 and 3x − y = 4.
  1. Add: 5x = 15, so x = 3.
  2. Substitute: 2(3) + y = 11, so y = 5.

Answer: x = 3, y = 5

The sum of two numbers is 20 and their difference is 6. Find them.
  1. x + y = 20 and x − y = 6.
  2. Add: 2x = 26, so x = 13; then y = 7.

Answer: 13 and 7
