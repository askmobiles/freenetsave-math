Angles in polygons

Any polygon can be cut into triangles from one corner. Each triangle holds 180°, so counting triangles gives the angle sum.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

The angles of a triangle add to 180°. Cut a polygon into triangles from one corner and count them.

There are always 2 fewer triangles than sides. So:

Angle sum = (sides − 2) × 180°

Regular polygons

In a regular polygon all angles are equal. Divide the sum by the number of sides. A regular hexagon: 720° ÷ 6 = 120° each.

Finding a missing angle

Add the angles you know and take them from the angle sum.

Quick check: a quadrilateral is 2 triangles, so its angles add to 360°.

See three worked examples

What do the angles in a hexagon add up to?
  1. A hexagon has 6 sides.
  2. (6 − 2) × 180 = 4 × 180.

Answer: 720°

Find each angle of a regular octagon.
  1. Angle sum: (8 − 2) × 180 = 1080°.
  2. Divide by 8 sides.

Answer: 135°

A quadrilateral has angles 90°, 85° and 110°. Find the fourth angle.
  1. The angles add to 360°.
  2. 90 + 85 + 110 = 285.
  3. 360 − 285.

Answer: 75°
