Multiplying decimals

To multiply decimals, multiply as if they were whole numbers. Then put the decimal point back in the right place.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

  1. Ignore the decimal points and multiply the whole numbers.
  2. Count the digits after the decimal point in both numbers and add the counts.
  3. Put the point in your answer so it has that many digits after it.
  4. Estimate to check that the answer is a sensible size.

For 4.6 × 0.3: multiply 46 × 3 = 138. There is one digit after the point in 4.6 and one in 0.3, so the answer needs two: 1.38.

Why does it work?

The square is one whole, cut into 100 small squares. The shaded strip going across is 0.4 of the square and the strip going down is 0.3. Where they overlap there are 12 small squares, which is 12 hundredths. So 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.

Tenths times tenths gives hundredths. That is why you add the decimal places.

Check with an estimate: 4.6 × 0.3 is about 5 × 0.3 = 1.5. The answer 1.38 is close, so the point is in the right place. An answer of 13.8 or 0.138 would be wrong.

See three worked examples

4.6 × 0.3
  1. Multiply without the points: 46 × 3 = 138.
  2. Count the decimal places: one in 4.6 and one in 0.3 makes two.
  3. Place the point so there are two digits after it.

Answer: 1.38

1.4 × 0.8
  1. Multiply without the points: 14 × 8 = 112.
  2. One decimal place in each number makes two.
  3. Check: 1.4 × 0.8 is a bit less than 1.4, and 1.12 fits.

Answer: 1.12

0.25 × 0.4
  1. Multiply without the points: 25 × 4 = 100.
  2. Two decimal places in 0.25 and one in 0.4 makes three: 0.100.
  3. Drop the zeros at the end.

Answer: 0.1
