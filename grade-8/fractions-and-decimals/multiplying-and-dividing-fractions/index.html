Multiplying and dividing fractions

Multiplying fractions is easier than adding them: no common denominator is needed. Dividing needs just one extra step.

Learn

Multiplying

Multiply the tops together and the bottoms together: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15. Then simplify: 6/15 = 2/5.

The word of means multiply. Half of 3/4 is 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8.

When you multiply by a fraction less than 1, the answer gets smaller. You are taking only part of the amount.

Dividing

Dividing asks "how many of these fit?" How many quarters fit into one half?

The shaded half is made of two quarter pieces, so the answer is 2.

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. The reciprocal is the fraction turned upside down: the reciprocal of 2/5 is 5/2.

  1. Keep the first fraction.
  2. Change ÷ to ×.
  3. Flip the second fraction.
  4. Multiply, then simplify.

Only the second fraction flips. 3/4 ÷ 2/5 becomes 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8.

See three worked examples

2/3 × 3/5
  1. Multiply the tops: 2 × 3 = 6.
  2. Multiply the bottoms: 3 × 5 = 15. That gives 6/15.
  3. Simplify: divide top and bottom by 3.

Answer: 2/5

3/4 ÷ 2/5
  1. Keep 3/4, change ÷ to ×, and flip 2/5 to 5/2.
  2. Multiply: 3 × 5 = 15 and 4 × 2 = 8.
  3. 15 and 8 share no factor, so it is already simplest.

Answer: 15/8

2/7 ÷ 1/7
  1. Keep 2/7, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/7 to 7/1.
  2. Multiply: 2 × 7 = 14 and 7 × 1 = 7. That gives 14/7.
  3. Simplify: 14 ÷ 7 = 2. Two sevenths holds exactly two pieces of one seventh.

Answer: 2
