Solving proportions

A proportion says that two ratios are equal. If you know three of the four numbers, you can find the fourth.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

"5 bus rides cost $40, so 9 rides cost how much?" is a proportion problem. The cost grows at the same rate as the number of rides.

The double number line shows the idea. First find the cost of 1 ride, then multiply up.

Three ways to solve

  1. Unit rate: find the amount for 1, then multiply. $40 ÷ 5 = $8 a ride, so 9 rides cost 9 × $8 = $72.
  2. Scale factor: if one number is multiplied by something, multiply the other by the same. 4 cost $12, so 8 cost $24.
  3. Cross-multiply: write it as fractions. x/6 = 10/15 gives 15x = 60, so x = 4.

Check that it really is a proportion. If more workers do a job, it takes less time, not more. That kind of problem is not solved by multiplying up.

See three worked examples

5 bus rides cost $40. How much do 9 rides cost?
  1. Unit rate: 40 ÷ 5 = $8 for one ride.
  2. Multiply up: 9 × 8 = 72.

Answer: $72

4 tickets cost $12. How much do 9 tickets cost?
  1. Unit rate: 12 ÷ 4 = $3 a ticket.
  2. 9 × 3 = 27.

Answer: $27

Solve x/6 = 10/15.
  1. Cross-multiply: 15 × x = 6 × 10.
  2. 15x = 60.
  3. Divide both sides by 15.

Answer: x = 4
