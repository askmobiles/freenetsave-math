Discounts and sale prices

A discount is a percent taken off the price. This lesson shows two ways to find the sale price.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Two-step way

  1. Find the discount: percent × price. For 40% off $200, that is 0.40 × 200 = $80.
  2. Subtract it from the price: 200 − 80 = $120.

One-step way

If 40% comes off, you pay the other 60%. So the sale price is 0.60 × 200 = $120. Subtract the percent from 100 first, then multiply once.

The shaded part of the bar is what you pay. The unshaded part, 40%, is the discount of $80.

Tax comes after the discount

Sales tax is worked out on the sale price, not the full price. To add 13% tax, multiply the sale price by 1.13.

Two discounts do not add. 20% off and then another 10% off is not 30% off. The second discount is taken from the lower price.

See three worked examples

A $200 coat is 40% off. What is the sale price?
  1. Discount: 0.40 × 200 = $80.
  2. Subtract: 200 − 80 = $120.
  3. Or in one step: you pay 60%, and 0.60 × 200 = $120.

Answer: $120

A $45 game is 20% off. What is the sale price?
  1. You pay 100% − 20% = 80%.
  2. Multiply: 0.80 × 45 = 36.

Answer: $36

An $80 pair of shoes is 15% off. What is the sale price?
  1. Discount: 0.15 × 80 = $12.
  2. Subtract: 80 − 12 = $68.

Answer: $68
