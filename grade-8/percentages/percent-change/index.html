Percent increase and decrease

Percent change tells you how big a change is compared with where you started.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A $10 rise matters a lot on a $20 item and very little on a $2,000 item. Percent change compares the change with the starting amount.

  1. Find the change: the difference between the new amount and the original amount.
  2. Divide the change by the original amount.
  3. Multiply by 100 to get a percent.

If the amount went up, it is a percent increase. If it went down, it is a percent decrease.

A price drops from $60 to $30. The change is $30. Compared with the original $60, that is 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5, a 50% decrease.

Always divide by the original amount, the one you started with, not the new one. This is the most common mistake.

Up, then down, is not back to the start

If $100 goes up 50% it becomes $150. If $150 then goes down 50% it becomes $75, not $100. The second percent is taken from a bigger amount.

See three worked examples

A price goes from $60 to $30. By what percent did it decrease?
  1. Change: 60 − 30 = 30.
  2. Divide by the original: 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5.
  3. Multiply by 100.

Answer: 50% decrease

A plant grows from 40 cm to 50 cm. What is the percent increase?
  1. Change: 50 − 40 = 10.
  2. Divide by the original: 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25.
  3. Multiply by 100.

Answer: 25% increase

A team scored 80 points last game and 68 this game. What is the percent decrease?
  1. Change: 80 − 68 = 12.
  2. Divide by the original: 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15.
  3. Multiply by 100.

Answer: 15% decrease
