The Pythagorean theorem: the long side

In any right triangle, the squares on the two short sides add up to the square on the long side.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Draw a square on each side of a right triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5. The two small squares hold 9 and 16. Together that is 25, exactly the big square.

The rule

a² + b² = c². The two short sides are a and b. The long side c is called the hypotenuse. It is always across from the square corner.

The steps

  1. Square both short sides.
  2. Add them.
  3. Take the square root.

Check your answer. The long side must be longer than each short side, but shorter than the two added together.

See three worked examples

A right triangle has short sides 3 and 4. Find the long side.
  1. 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25.
  2. The square root of 25.

Answer: 5

A right triangle has short sides 8 and 15. Find the long side.
  1. 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289.
  2. The square root of 289.

Answer: 17

A right triangle has short sides 5 and 12. Find the long side.
  1. 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169.
  2. The square root of 169.

Answer: 13
