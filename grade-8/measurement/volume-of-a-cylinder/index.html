Volume of a cylinder

A cylinder is a stack of circles. Find the area of one circle, then multiply by the height.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A can of soup is a cylinder. Its base is a circle with area πr². Stack that circle up to the height h and you fill the can.

The steps

  1. Square the radius.
  2. Multiply by 3.14 to get the base area.
  3. Multiply by the height.

Radius 3 cm, height 10 cm: 3 × 3 = 9, 9 × 3.14 = 28.26, and 28.26 × 10 = 282.6 cm³.

Volume is in cubic units such as cm³. For liquids, 1 cm³ = 1 mL and 1 m³ = 1,000 L.

Watch for the diameter. If a can is 10 cm across, its radius is 5 cm.

See three worked examples

Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm.
  1. Base: 3 × 3 × 3.14 = 28.26.
  2. 28.26 × 10.

Answer: 282.6 cm³

Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 2 m and height 5 m.
  1. Base: 2 × 2 × 3.14 = 12.56.
  2. 12.56 × 5.

Answer: 62.8 m³

A can is 10 cm across and 4 cm tall. Find its volume.
  1. Radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5.
  2. Base: 5 × 5 × 3.14 = 78.5.
  3. 78.5 × 4.

Answer: 314 cm³
