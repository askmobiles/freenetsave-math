Area of a circle

The area is the space inside the circle. Square the radius, then multiply by π.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Cut a circle into thin slices and lay them top to bottom. They make a shape that is almost a rectangle.

The rectangle is half the circumference long (πr) and one radius tall (r). So its area is πr × r = πr².

The formula

A = π × r × r. Square the radius first, then multiply by 3.14.

A circle with radius 4 cm: 4 × 4 = 16, and 16 × 3.14 = 50.24 cm².

Area is in square units, such as cm² or m², because it counts squares.

Given the diameter? Halve it to get the radius first. A diameter of 10 means r = 5, so A = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5.

See three worked examples

Find the area of a circle with radius 4 cm.
  1. Square the radius: 4 × 4 = 16.
  2. 16 × 3.14 = 50.24.

Answer: 50.24 cm²

Find the area of a circle with diameter 10 m.
  1. Radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5.
  2. 5 × 5 = 25.
  3. 25 × 3.14 = 78.5.

Answer: 78.5 m²

Find the area of a circle with radius 1.5 cm.
  1. 1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25.
  2. 2.25 × 3.14 = 7.065.

Answer: 7.065 cm²
