Circumference of a circle

The circumference is the distance all the way around a circle. It is always a little more than 3 times the distance across.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Every circle has a centre. The distance from the centre to the edge is the radius (r). The distance right across, through the centre, is the diameter (d). The diameter is two radii, so d = 2r.

If you wrap a string around any circle and lay it straight, it is about 3.14 times as long as the diameter. That number is called π (pi).

The formula

C = π × d, or, using the radius, C = 2 × π × r. In these lessons we use π = 3.14.

A circle with radius 2 cm: C = 2 × 3.14 × 2 = 12.56 cm.

Read the question carefully. If it gives the diameter, use C = π × d. If it gives the radius, double it first or use C = 2πr.

See three worked examples

A circle has radius 2 cm. Find its circumference.
  1. C = 2 × π × r.
  2. 2 × 3.14 × 2 = 12.56.

Answer: 12.56 cm

A circle has diameter 10 m. Find its circumference.
  1. With the diameter, C = π × d.
  2. 3.14 × 10 = 31.4.

Answer: 31.4 m

A round table has radius 7 dm. How far is it around the edge?
  1. C = 2 × 3.14 × 7.
  2. 2 × 7 = 14, and 14 × 3.14 = 43.96.

Answer: 43.96 dm
