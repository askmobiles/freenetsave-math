Adding and subtracting integers

Integers are the whole numbers, their negatives, and zero. A number line makes adding and subtracting them easy to picture.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

On a number line, numbers get greater as you move right and smaller as you move left.

  • Adding a positive number moves you to the right.
  • Adding a negative number moves you to the left.

The picture shows −7 + 4. Start at −7 and move 4 steps to the right. You land on −3.

Subtracting is adding the opposite

Every integer has an opposite: the opposite of 3 is −3, and the opposite of −3 is 3. To subtract a number, add its opposite instead.

  • 5 − 8 becomes 5 + (−8) = −3.
  • 5 − (−3) becomes 5 + 3 = 8. Taking away a debt makes you richer.

Zero pairs

A number and its opposite add to zero: 6 + (−6) = 0. Spotting these pairs in a long sum saves work.

Two quick rules: same signs, add the sizes and keep the sign. Different signs, subtract the sizes and keep the sign of the bigger size.

See three worked examples

−7 + 4
  1. The signs are different, so subtract the sizes: 7 − 4 = 3.
  2. The bigger size is 7, and it is negative, so the answer is negative.

Answer: −3

5 − (−3)
  1. Subtracting is adding the opposite. The opposite of −3 is 3.
  2. So 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3.
  3. Add: 5 + 3 = 8.

Answer: 8

−6 − 9 + 2
  1. Change the subtraction: −6 + (−9) + 2.
  2. Same signs first: −6 + (−9) = −15.
  3. Then −15 + 2: different signs, 15 − 2 = 13, and the bigger size is negative.

Answer: −13
