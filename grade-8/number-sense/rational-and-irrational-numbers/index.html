Rational and irrational numbers

Every number on the number line is either rational or irrational. This lesson shows how to tell which is which.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A rational number is any number you can write as a fraction of two integers, where the bottom number is not zero. The word comes from ratio.

  • Whole numbers and integers are rational: 5 = 5/1 and −3 = −3/1.
  • Fractions are rational: 2/3, −7/4.
  • Decimals that stop are rational: 0.75 = 3/4.
  • Decimals that repeat forever are rational: 0.333… = 1/3.

An irrational number cannot be written as a fraction of two integers. Its decimal goes on forever and never settles into a repeating pattern. Two famous ones are π = 3.14159… and √2 = 1.41421…

The square root test

A square root is rational only when the number under the root sign is a perfect square such as 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 or 100. So √16 = 4 is rational, but √15 and √17 are irrational.

The picture shows rational numbers in blue and irrational numbers in orange. Both kinds sit on the same number line. Together they are called the real numbers.

Remember: if you can write it as a fraction of integers, it is rational. A decimal that never ends and never repeats is irrational.

See three worked examples

Is 0.75 rational or irrational?
  1. The decimal stops after two places.
  2. Write it as a fraction: 0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4.
  3. It is a fraction of two integers.

Answer: rational

Which is irrational: √25 or √24?
  1. 25 is a perfect square, because 5 × 5 = 25. So √25 = 5, which is rational.
  2. 24 is not a perfect square: 4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25, and 24 is between them.
  3. So √24 is a decimal that never ends or repeats (about 4.899).

Answer: √24 is irrational

How many of these are irrational?   √9   √10   2/3   π
  1. √9 = 3, so it is rational.
  2. 10 is not a perfect square, so √10 is irrational.
  3. 2/3 is a fraction, so it is rational, even though 0.666… goes on forever.
  4. π is irrational.

Answer: 2 of them (√10 and π)
