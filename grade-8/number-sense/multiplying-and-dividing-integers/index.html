Multiplying and dividing integers

Multiplying and dividing integers uses the facts you already know. The only new part is deciding the sign.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Work out the size of the answer as usual, then choose the sign:

  • Same signs give a positive answer: (−4) × (−5) = 20 and 20 ÷ 5 = 4.
  • Different signs give a negative answer: (−4) × 5 = −20 and 20 ÷ (−5) = −4.

Why do two negatives make a positive?

Look at the pattern as the first number goes down by 1 each time:

  • 2 × (−3) = −6
  • 1 × (−3) = −3
  • 0 × (−3) = 0
  • (−1) × (−3) = 3
  • (−2) × (−3) = 6

Each answer is 3 more than the one before. To keep the pattern going past zero, the answers must become positive.

More than two numbers

Count the negative signs. An even number of negatives gives a positive answer. An odd number gives a negative answer. So (−2)³ = −8, but (−2)⁴ = 16.

Careful: these sign rules are for multiplying and dividing only. Adding is different: −3 + (−4) is −7, not 7.

See three worked examples

(−4) × 6
  1. Size: 4 × 6 = 24.
  2. The signs are different, so the answer is negative.

Answer: −24

(−5) × (−7)
  1. Size: 5 × 7 = 35.
  2. The signs are the same, so the answer is positive.

Answer: 35

−48 ÷ (−6) × (−2)
  1. Work from left to right. First −48 ÷ (−6): size 8, same signs, so 8.
  2. Then 8 × (−2): size 16, different signs.

Answer: −16
