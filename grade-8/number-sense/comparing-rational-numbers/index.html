Comparing and ordering rational numbers

Which is greater, 3/4 or 0.7? What about −1/2 and −0.6? Put the numbers in the same form and the answer is easy to see.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Fractions and decimals are hard to compare when they look different. Pick one form and change every number into it.

Method 1: change everything to decimals

Divide the top of the fraction by the bottom: 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. Then line up the decimal points and compare place by place, starting from the left.

Method 2: use a common denominator

To compare 2/3 and 3/5, write both over 15: 2/3 = 10/15 and 3/5 = 9/15. Since 10 is more than 9, 2/3 is greater.

Negative numbers

On a number line, the number further to the right is always greater. For negative numbers, that is the one closer to zero.

In the picture, −1/2 is to the right of −0.6, so −1/2 is greater, even though 0.6 is a bigger size than 0.5.

Symbols: > means "is greater than" and < means "is less than". The open side always faces the greater number: 3/4 > 0.7 and −0.6 < −1/2.

See three worked examples

Which is greater: 3/4 or 0.7?
  1. Change the fraction to a decimal: 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.
  2. Compare 0.75 and 0.70. The tenths are both 7. The hundredths are 5 and 0.
  3. 5 is more than 0, so 0.75 is greater.

Answer: 3/4

Which is greater: −1/2 or −0.6?
  1. Change to decimals: −1/2 = −0.5.
  2. Both are negative. The one closer to zero is greater.
  3. −0.5 is closer to zero than −0.6.

Answer: −1/2

Put in order from least to greatest: −1.2, −5/4, 1.1
  1. Change to decimals: −5/4 = −1.25.
  2. The negatives come first. −1.25 is further left than −1.2, so it is the least.
  3. The only positive number, 1.1, is the greatest.

Answer: −5/4, −1.2, 1.1
