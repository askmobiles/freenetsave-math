Finding the value of an expression

An expression is like a recipe with a blank in it. Put a number in for the letter and you can work out its value.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

In the expression 3x + 4, the letter x stands for a number we have not chosen yet. 3x means 3 × x.

To substitute means to put a number in place of the letter. When x = 5:

3x + 4 → 3 × 5 + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19

Do it in the right order

  1. Brackets first.
  2. Then powers, such as x².
  3. Then multiply and divide.
  4. Then add and subtract.

Negative numbers

Put a negative number in brackets when you substitute it. If x = −2, then x² = (−2)² = (−2) × (−2) = 4, not −4.

3x is not thirty-something. When x = 5, 3x means 3 × 5 = 15. It is never 35.

See three worked examples

Find 3x + 4 when x = 5.
  1. Put 5 in place of x: 3 × 5 + 4.
  2. Multiply first: 15 + 4.
  3. Then add.

Answer: 19

Find 2x² − 7 when x = 3.
  1. Power first: 3² = 9.
  2. Multiply: 2 × 9 = 18.
  3. Subtract: 18 − 7.

Answer: 11

Find 5a − 2b when a = 4 and b = −3.
  1. 5 × 4 = 20.
  2. 2 × (−3) = −6.
  3. 20 − (−6) is the same as 20 + 6.

Answer: 26
