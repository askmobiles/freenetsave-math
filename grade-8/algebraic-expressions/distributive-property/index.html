Expanding brackets

The number outside the brackets multiplies everything inside the brackets, not just the first term.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

3(2x + 5) means 3 groups of (2x + 5). An area model shows why both terms get multiplied.

The rectangle is 3 tall. One part is 2x wide, so its area is 3 × 2x = 6x. The other part is 5 wide, so its area is 3 × 5 = 15. Together: 3(2x + 5) = 6x + 15.

Watch the signs

  • 4(x − 3) = 4x − 12
  • −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10, because −2 × −5 = +10
  • x(x + 2) = x² + 2x

Expand, then simplify

After expanding, collect any like terms. 2(x + 4) + 3x = 2x + 8 + 3x = 5x + 8.

The most common mistake: 3(x + 5) is not 3x + 5. The 3 multiplies the 5 too, so it is 3x + 15.

See three worked examples

Expand 3(2x + 5).
  1. 3 × 2x = 6x.
  2. 3 × 5 = 15.

Answer: 6x + 15

Expand 4(3x − 2).
  1. 4 × 3x = 12x.
  2. 4 × (−2) = −8.

Answer: 12x − 8

Expand and simplify 2(x + 4) + 3x.
  1. 2(x + 4) = 2x + 8.
  2. Add the x terms: 2x + 3x = 5x.

Answer: 5x + 8
