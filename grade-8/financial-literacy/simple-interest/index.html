Simple interest

When you save money, the bank pays you extra called interest. Simple interest is the same amount every year.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

You put $1,000 in a savings account that pays 5% a year. Each year you earn 5% of $1,000, which is $50.

The formula

Interest = amount × rate × years ÷ 100. The amount you start with is called the principal.

$1,000 × 5 × 3 ÷ 100 = $150 after 3 years, so you have $1,150 in all.

A loan works the same way, but you pay the interest to the bank.

Interest or total? Read the question. The interest is only the extra. The total is the principal plus the interest.

See three worked examples

$1,800 at 1% a year for 2 years. How much interest?
  1. 1,800 × 1 = 1,800.
  2. 1,800 × 2 = 3,600.
  3. 3,600 ÷ 100.

Answer: $36

$500 at 4% a year for 3 years. How much interest?
  1. 500 × 4 × 3 = 6,000.
  2. 6,000 ÷ 100.

Answer: $60

$2,000 at 3.5% a year for 2 years. What is the total?
  1. Interest: 2,000 × 3.5 × 2 ÷ 100 = 140.
  2. Total: 2,000 + 140.

Answer: $2,140
