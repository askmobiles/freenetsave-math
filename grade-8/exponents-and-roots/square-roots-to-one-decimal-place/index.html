Square roots to one decimal place

You can find a square root to one decimal place with no calculator, just by squaring a few decimals.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

  1. Find the two whole numbers the root is between.
  2. Guess a decimal in between and square it.
  3. Adjust up or down until you find the two tenths it falls between.
  4. Use the halfway check to pick the closer one.

For √68: it is between 8 and 9. Try 8.2² = 67.24, which is too small, and 8.3² = 68.89, which is too big. So √68 is between 8.2 and 8.3.

Halfway check: 8.25² = 68.0625. 68 is just below that, so √68 rounds to 8.2.

Check your answer by squaring it. 8.2² = 67.24, which is close to 68. If the square is far from the number, try again.

See three worked examples

Find √68 to one decimal place.
  1. √68 is between 8 and 9.
  2. 8.2² = 67.24 and 8.3² = 68.89, so it is between 8.2 and 8.3.
  3. Halfway: 8.25² = 68.0625. 68 is below it.

Answer: 8.2

Find √50 to one decimal place.
  1. √50 is between 7 and 8. 7² = 49 is very close, so try 7.1.
  2. 7.1² = 50.41, a little too big. 7.0² = 49.
  3. Halfway: 7.05² = 49.7025. 50 is above it.

Answer: 7.1

Find √20 to one decimal place.
  1. √20 is between 4 and 5.
  2. 4.4² = 19.36 and 4.5² = 20.25.
  3. Halfway: 4.45² = 19.8025. 20 is above it.

Answer: 4.5
