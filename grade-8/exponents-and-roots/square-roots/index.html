Square roots of perfect squares

A square root undoes squaring. It asks: what number times itself gives this?

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

6² = 36, so the square root of 36 is 6. We write √36 = 6.

Think of a square made of small tiles. If the square has an area of 36 tiles, each side is 6 tiles long. The square root of the area is the side length.

Perfect squares to know

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225, 400. These have whole-number square roots.

Finding a bigger square root

Use what you know. √324 must be between 10 and 20, because 10² = 100 and 20² = 400. It ends in 4, so the root ends in 2 or 8. Try 18: 18 × 18 = 324.

Squaring and square-rooting undo each other: √(9²) = 9 and (√9)² = 9.

See three worked examples

√144
  1. Which number times itself is 144?
  2. 12 × 12 = 144.

Answer: 12

√324
  1. 10² = 100 and 20² = 400, so the root is between 10 and 20.
  2. 324 ends in 4, so try numbers ending in 2 or 8.
  3. 18 × 18 = 324.

Answer: 18

√400
  1. 20 × 20 = 400.

Answer: 20
