Exponent rules

When powers have the same base, three short rules let you combine them without writing out every factor.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

1. Multiplying: add the exponents

x³ · x⁵ = (x · x · x) · (x · x · x · x · x) = x⁸. There are 3 + 5 = 8 factors in all.

2. Dividing: subtract the exponents

x⁶ ÷ x² = x⁴. Two of the x's on top cancel with the two on the bottom, leaving 6 − 2 = 4.

3. Power of a power: multiply the exponents

(x²)⁴ = x² · x² · x² · x² = x⁸, because there are 4 groups of 2.

The zero exponent

x⁵ ÷ x⁵ = 1, and by the subtraction rule it is also x⁰. So any number (except 0) to the power 0 is 1.

The rules only work when the bases are the same. 2³ · 3² cannot be combined into one power. Work each part out instead: 8 × 9 = 72.

See three worked examples

x⁶ ÷ x²
  1. Same base, dividing: subtract the exponents.
  2. 6 − 2 = 4.

Answer: x⁴

x³ · x⁵
  1. Same base, multiplying: add the exponents.
  2. 3 + 5 = 8.

Answer: x⁸

(x²)⁴
  1. A power of a power: multiply the exponents.
  2. 2 × 4 = 8.

Answer: x⁸
