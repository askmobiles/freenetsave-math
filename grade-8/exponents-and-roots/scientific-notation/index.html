Scientific notation

Scientists write huge and tiny numbers in a short form that is easy to read and compare.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A number in scientific notation has two parts: a number from 1 up to (but not including) 10, times a power of 10.

68,000 = 6.8 × 10⁴

From scientific notation to an ordinary number

The power of 10 tells you how many places to move the decimal point. A positive power moves it to the right.

From an ordinary number to scientific notation

Move the decimal point until one non-zero digit is in front of it. Count the moves: that is the power. 3,200,000 becomes 3.2 × 10⁶, because the point moved 6 places.

Very small numbers

A negative power moves the point to the left: 4.5 × 10⁻³ = 0.0045.

Check the first part. 32 × 10⁵ is not in scientific notation, because 32 is 10 or more. It should be 3.2 × 10⁶.

See three worked examples

Write 6.8 × 10⁴ as an ordinary number.
  1. The power is 4, so move the decimal point 4 places to the right.
  2. 6.8 → 68 → 680 → 6,800 → 68,000.

Answer: 68,000

Write 3,200,000 in scientific notation.
  1. Put the point after the first digit: 3.2.
  2. The point moved 6 places to the left to get there.

Answer: 3.2 × 10⁶

Write 4.5 × 10⁻³ as an ordinary number.
  1. A negative power: move the point 3 places to the left.
  2. 4.5 → 0.45 → 0.045 → 0.0045.

Answer: 0.0045
