Powers and exponents

A power is a short way to write a number multiplied by itself many times.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

In 3⁴, the base is 3 and the exponent is 4. The exponent tells you how many times the base is used as a factor:

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81

It is not 3 × 4. That is the most common mistake with powers.

Powers of 2 double each time

Each new power is the one before it, multiplied by the base again.

Special powers

  • Any number to the power 1 is itself: 7¹ = 7.
  • Powers of 10 are 1 followed by that many zeros: 10⁴ = 10,000.
  • 1 to any power is 1.

Negative bases

(−2)⁴ = (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = 16. But −2⁴ means −(2⁴) = −16. The brackets decide what the power belongs to.

See three worked examples

3⁴
  1. Use 3 as a factor 4 times: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3.
  2. 3 × 3 = 9, 9 × 3 = 27, 27 × 3 = 81.

Answer: 81

2⁵
  1. 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2.
  2. 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.

Answer: 32

(−3)³
  1. (−3) × (−3) × (−3).
  2. (−3) × (−3) = 9. Then 9 × (−3) = −27.
  3. Three negatives is an odd number, so the answer is negative.

Answer: −27
