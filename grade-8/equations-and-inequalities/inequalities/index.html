Solving inequalities

An inequality says one side is bigger or smaller, not equal. Solve it like an equation, then read the sign.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

The signs are: > greater than, < less than, ≥ greater than or equal to, ≤ less than or equal to.

Solve 3x + 2 > 14 the same way as an equation: take 2 from both sides (3x > 12), then divide by 3: x > 4.

x must be more than 4, so 4 itself does not work. The smallest whole number that works is 5.

With "or equal to"

2x − 1 ≥ 11 gives 2x ≥ 12, so x ≥ 6. This time 6 itself works, so the smallest whole number is 6.

Multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips the sign. −2x > 6 becomes x < −3. Check: x = −4 gives −2 × −4 = 8, and 8 > 6 is true.

See three worked examples

3x + 2 > 14. What is the smallest whole number x can be?
  1. Take 2 from both sides: 3x > 12.
  2. Divide by 3: x > 4.
  3. x must be more than 4.

Answer: 5

2x − 1 ≥ 11. What is the smallest whole number x can be?
  1. Add 1 to both sides: 2x ≥ 12.
  2. Divide by 2: x ≥ 6.
  3. 6 itself works because of "or equal to".

Answer: 6

Solve −2x > 6.
  1. Divide both sides by −2.
  2. Dividing by a negative number flips the sign: > becomes <.

Answer: x < −3
