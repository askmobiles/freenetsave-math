The discriminant

b² − 4ac tells you how many times the parabola crosses the x-axis.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

For ax² + bx + c = 0, the discriminant is D = b² − 4ac. It comes from the quadratic formula, x = (−b ± √D) ÷ 2a.

  • D > 0: two real roots (crosses twice).
  • D = 0: one real root (touches once).
  • D < 0: no real roots (never touches).

x² + 3x − 4 = 0: D = 9 − 4(1)(−4) = 25 > 0, two roots.

Careful with signs: −4ac with c negative becomes positive.

See three worked examples

x² − 6x + 9 = 0
  1. D = 36 − 36 = 0.

Answer: one root

2x² + x + 5 = 0
  1. D = 1 − 40 = −39.

Answer: no real roots

3x² − 2x − 1 = 0
  1. D = 4 + 12 = 16.

Answer: two roots
