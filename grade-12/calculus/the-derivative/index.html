The derivative: slope of a curve

The derivative f′(x) gives the slope of the curve at each point.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

A curve has a different slope at each point. The tangent line just touches the curve and has that slope.

The derivative is the limit of secant slopes: f′(a) = lim h→0 of [f(a + h) − f(a)] ÷ h. For f(x) = x² this gives f′(x) = 2x, so the slope at x = 2 is 4.

For polynomials, term by term

f(x) = x³ − 2x² + 5x: f′(x) = 3x² − 4x + 5. At x = 1: 3 − 4 + 5 = 4.

Meaning: f′ is the instantaneous rate of change: speed from distance, marginal cost from cost.

See three worked examples

f(x) = x². f′(3)?
  1. f′(x) = 2x.

Answer: 6

f(x) = x³ + x. f′(2)?
  1. 3x² + 1 = 13.

Answer: 13

f(x) = 5x − 7. f′(x)?
  1. A line has constant slope.

Answer: 5
