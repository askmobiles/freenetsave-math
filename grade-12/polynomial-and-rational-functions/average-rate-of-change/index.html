Average rate of change

Average rate of change = change in y ÷ change in x, between two points on a curve.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

For f(x) = x², from x = 1 to x = 3: (9 − 1) ÷ (3 − 1) = 4. That is the slope of the secant line joining the two points.

(f(b) − f(a)) ÷ (b − a)

Meaning

If d(t) is distance in km and t is hours, the average rate of change is the average speed. If P(t) is a population, it is the average growth per year.

Instantaneous rate is the slope at one point. You get it by moving the two points closer and closer — that is the start of calculus.

See three worked examples

f(x) = x², from 2 to 5
  1. (25 − 4) ÷ 3.

Answer: 7

f(x) = x³, from 0 to 2
  1. (8 − 0) ÷ 2.

Answer: 4

A car is at 40 km at t = 1 h and 220 km at t = 3 h.
  1. 180 ÷ 2.

Answer: 90 km/h
