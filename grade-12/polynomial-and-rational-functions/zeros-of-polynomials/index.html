Zeros of polynomial functions

Each factor (x − r) gives a zero at x = r.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

f(x) = x(x − 3)(x + 2) has zeros at x = 0, 3 and −2.

Repeated zeros

  • (x − 1)²: the graph touches the x-axis at 1 and bounces back (order 2).
  • (x − 1)³: it flattens and passes through at 1 (order 3).

y-intercept

Put x = 0. For f(x) = 2(x − 1)(x + 4): 2(−1)(4) = −8.

Factor first: x³ − 4x = x(x − 2)(x + 2), so the zeros are 0, 2 and −2.

See three worked examples

Zeros of (x − 5)(x + 1)(2x − 3)
  1. 2x − 3 = 0 gives 3/2.

Answer: 5, −1, 3/2

Zeros of x³ − 9x
  1. x(x − 3)(x + 3).

Answer: 0, 3, −3

What happens at x = 2 for (x − 2)²(x + 1)?
  1. Order 2.

Answer: touches and bounces
