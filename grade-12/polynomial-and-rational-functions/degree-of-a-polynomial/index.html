Degree and end behaviour of polynomials

The degree is the highest power of x. It controls the shape and the ends of the graph.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

f(x) = 4x⁵ − 2x² + 7 has degree 5 and leading coefficient 4.

Degree after multiplying

(x³ + 2)(x² − 5): the highest power comes from x³ × x² = x⁵, so the degree is 5. Add the degrees of the factors.

End behaviour

  • Odd degree, positive leading coefficient: falls on the left, rises on the right (like y = x³).
  • Even degree, positive: both ends rise (like y = x²).
  • A negative leading coefficient flips both ends.

Turning points: a polynomial of degree n has at most n − 1 turns and at most n zeros.

See three worked examples

Degree of 3x⁴ − x⁷ + 2
  1. Highest power 7.

Answer: 7

Degree of (x² + 1)(x³ − 4)(x − 2)
  1. 2 + 3 + 1.

Answer: 6

Ends of y = −2x³ + x
  1. Odd degree, negative leading coefficient.

Answer: rises on the left, falls on the right
