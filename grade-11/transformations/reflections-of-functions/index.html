Reflections in the axes

−f(x) flips over the x-axis. f(−x) flips over the y-axis.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn

Blue: y = √x. Orange: y = −√x (flipped down). Green: y = √(−x) (flipped left).

  • y = −f(x): (x, y) → (x, −y). Over the x-axis.
  • y = f(−x): (x, y) → (−x, y). Over the y-axis.

Symmetry

If f(−x) = f(x), the graph is its own mirror image in the y-axis (like y = x²). Such functions are called even.

The minus sign's place tells you the axis: outside → x-axis, inside → y-axis.

See three worked examples

(5, 3) on f. On −f(x)?
  1. y changes sign.

Answer: (5, −3)

(5, 3) on f. On f(−x)?
  1. x changes sign.

Answer: (−5, 3)

(5, 3) on f. On −f(−x)?
  1. Both change.

Answer: (−5, −3)
